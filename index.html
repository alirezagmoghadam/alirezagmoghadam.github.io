Current work

2026–27

Master's thesis: randomization tests for replicated single-case experiments

KU Leuven, supervised by Prof. Patrick Onghena. In progress.

A Monte Carlo study of the Type I error rate and power of randomization tests that combine evidence across replicated randomized AB (single-case) designs. Earlier simulation work modelled randomization-test P values as continuous under the null hypothesis, although they can only take discrete values. This thesis tests whether those results hold once the discreteness is taken into account, and compares combining P values against combining the random assignments directly.

Full title: A statistical comparison of randomization tests for testing an intervention effect in replicated randomized AB designs: the combination puzzle.

2023–24

Multimodal fusion of clinical time series, chest X-rays and clinical notes

B.Sc. thesis, Ferdowsi University of Mashhad, supervised by Prof. Hadi Sadoghi Yazdi.

A fusion network trained on 6,125 intensive care patients from MIMIC-IV, MIMIC-CXR and MIMIC-IV-Note. Each modality has its own encoder, pretrained separately, and their features are then fused for prediction. Training with multimodal dropout lets the model still predict when a modality is missing.

2024–25

Automatic pain detection from facial expressions

Research intern, Ferdowsi University of Mashhad.

A real-time Python pipeline that scores pain frame by frame from video: face detection with RetinaFace, landmark localisation with MobileFaceNet and facial action unit estimation with Py-Feat, combined into the Prkachin and Solomon Pain Intensity score over AU4, AU6/7, AU9/10 and AU43 and thresholded for a per-frame pain decision at five processed frames per second.

The pipeline ran as the pain-detection module of an ESP32-based knee therapy device, which stops and raises an alert when it detects pain. The project also reviewed the annotated pain corpora available for supervised training (UNBC-McMaster, BioVid Heat Pain, BP4D+, Pain Emotion Faces Database) for patients who cannot self-report.
