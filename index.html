About

I am a second-year master's student in Statistics and Data Science at KU Leuven, Belgium. Before that I completed a B.Sc. in Computer Science and Engineering at Ferdowsi University of Mashhad, Iran.

I work on machine learning for clinical decision support, in particular multimodal models that combine structured electronic health record data, medical imaging and free-text clinical notes in a single predictive model. I am interested in how these models behave when a modality is missing at prediction time, and in principled statistical evaluation of predictive models in medical settings.

Current work

Master's thesis: randomization tests for replicated single-case experiments

KU Leuven, supervised by Prof. Patrick Onghena · 2026–2027, in progress

A Monte Carlo study of the Type I error rate and power of randomization tests that combine evidence across replicated randomized AB (single-case) designs. Earlier simulation work modelled randomization-test P values as continuous under the null hypothesis, although they can only take discrete values. This thesis tests whether those results hold once the discreteness is taken into account, and compares combining P values against combining the random assignments directly.

Full title: A statistical comparison of randomization tests for testing an intervention effect in replicated randomized AB designs: the combination puzzle.

Multimodal fusion of clinical time series, chest X-ray images and clinical notes

B.Sc. thesis, Ferdowsi University of Mashhad, supervised by Prof. Hadi Sadoghi Yazdi · 2023–2024

A three-modality fusion network trained on 6,125 intensive care patients from MIMIC-IV, MIMIC-CXR and MIMIC-IV-Note. Clinical time series are encoded by a stacked LSTM, chest radiographs by a ResNet-34 pretrained on CheXpert, and discharge and radiology notes by BioWordVec embeddings and a second LSTM. The encoders are pretrained separately and then fused. Training with multimodal dropout (p = 0.3) makes the model usable when a modality is missing at prediction time.

Automatic pain detection from facial expressions

Research intern, Ferdowsi University of Mashhad · 2024–2025

A real-time Python pipeline that scores pain frame by frame from video: face detection with RetinaFace, landmark localisation with MobileFaceNet and facial action unit estimation with Py-Feat, combined into the Prkachin and Solomon Pain Intensity score over AU4, AU6/7, AU9/10 and AU43 and thresholded for a per-frame pain decision at five processed frames per second. The pipeline ran as the pain-detection module of an ESP32-based knee therapy device, which stops and raises an alert when it detects pain. The project included a review of annotated pain corpora (UNBC-McMaster, BioVid Heat Pain, BP4D+, Pain Emotion Faces Database) for patients who cannot self-report.
